Questions and answers

Short answers about Romlin. If yours isn't here, ask in Romlin's issues on GitHub.

What is Romlin?

Romlin is a free, open source desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux that plays the games in your RomM library. You choose a game and press Play; Romlin downloads the game and the right emulator, and syncs your saves with RomM.

What is RomM?

RomM is a self-hosted server for your own game collection. It organises your games with covers and details, and stores saves. Romlin is a client for it: it needs a RomM server, version 5.0 or newer.

Is Romlin free?

Yes, and it always will be. There are no paid features, subscriptions, ads, tracking or accounts of its own. Romlin is licensed under the GPL, version 3 or later.

Does Romlin include games or BIOS files?

No. You need your own games and BIOS files on your RomM server. Romlin takes BIOS files from your server's firmware.

Which systems does Romlin play?

More than 50, from the Atari 2600 to the PlayStation 3 and original Xbox, plus arcade games and home computers such as the Amiga, Commodore 64 and DOS. See supported systems.

Do I need to install or set up emulators?

No. Romlin downloads the emulator for a system the first time you play one of its games. Most are libretro cores; Xbox games use xemu and PS3 games use RPCS3.

Does Romlin sync my saves?

Yes. In-game saves and save states sync with RomM before and after you play. If a save changed on two devices, Romlin asks which to keep and backs up the other. See save sync.

Can I play offline?

Yes. Games you've downloaded play without the server, and saves made offline sync the next time Romlin reaches RomM.

Does Romlin support RetroAchievements?

Yes. Sign in with your RetroAchievements account to earn achievements as you play, with popups, leaderboards, and a list on each game's page. Hardcore mode comes once RetroAchievements approves Romlin, which it considers after an emulator has been public for six months; until then, unlocks count as casual.

Does it work with a controller?

Yes. Gamepads are picked up automatically and join as the next player, any key or button can be remapped, and the whole app can be used with a controller. Light guns, mice and keyboards work for the systems that use them.

How is Romlin different from RetroArch?

RetroArch is a general emulator front end that you set up yourself. Romlin uses many of the same libretro cores, but is built around a RomM library: it shows your server's games, downloads what you play, picks and sets up the emulator, and syncs your saves with RomM.

How does Romlin compare with OpenEmu?

OpenEmu is a Mac app that brought an “it just works” approach to emulation: you drag your games in, it organises them, and you plug in a controller and play. Romlin is inspired by that approach. The differences are that Romlin runs on Windows and Linux as well as macOS, and that your games come from your RomM server instead of a library on one computer, so saves and save states follow you between computers through RomM.

Is Romlin safe to run with games and emulators from the internet?

Each game runs in its own sandboxed process that can only write to that game's saves and its own temporary files, so a buggy or malicious emulator or ROM can't touch your other files or take Romlin down with it. xemu and RPCS3 run outside the sandbox.

Can I send a game from RomM to Romlin?

Yes, with RomM 5.4. Choose Install on device in RomM and pick Romlin; the game starts downloading straight away, or the next time Romlin is open.

Does Romlin send any data about me?

No. Romlin has no servers of its own and no analytics. It talks only to your RomM server, RetroAchievements if you sign in, and the places it downloads emulators and updates from. Its privacy policy lists everything.

Which languages is Romlin in?

Romlin is available in English.

Is there a version for phones or the web?

Not for now: Romlin is a desktop app for macOS, Windows and Linux. A full-screen TV mode is planned, for a computer under the TV that you use with only a controller, which also suits any computer set aside just for games. On Android, Argosy is RomM's own app, and RomM has a web player built in.
